Pressure-Treated Pine Decking

The most widely used decking material, and the budget-friendly starting point for most projects.

Why Homeowners Choose It

  • The lowest upfront cost of any decking material
  • Treated to resist rot and insects
  • Real wood look that can be stained in many colors
  • Easy to build with, so projects move along quickly

Good For

  • Homeowners working with a tighter budget
  • Large decks, where material costs add up fast
  • Owners who don't mind regular maintenance
  • Decks you may want to restain a different color later

Lifespan & Upkeep

  • Lifespan: about 10 to 20 years with good care
  • Upkeep: clean once or twice a year, and seal or stain every 2 to 3 years
  • New boards: let them dry for several weeks before staining

Keep in Mind

  • Can warp, crack, and splinter as it dries and weathers
  • Turns gray if it isn't sealed regularly
  • Upkeep costs add up over the years

Cedar Decking

A naturally beautiful wood with a warm color, a clean grain, and a comfortable feel underfoot.

Why Homeowners Choose It

  • Rich natural color and grain
  • Naturally resists decay and insects
  • Stays cooler on bare feet than composite
  • Can be finished to keep its color, or left to weather to a soft silver-gray

Good For

  • Homeowners who want the look of real wood and will care for it
  • Sunny decks where bare feet matter
  • Anyone who wants a more upscale natural look than pine

Lifespan & Upkeep

  • Lifespan: about 15 to 30 years depending on care and exposure
  • Upkeep: clean yearly, and refinish every couple of years to keep the color, or let it age gray
  • Cost: moderately more than pressure-treated pine

Keep in Mind

  • Softer wood, so it dents and scratches more easily
  • Can fade, check, and cup without finishing
  • Costs more than pressure-treated pine

Ipe & Tropical Hardwood Decking

Premium, extremely dense hardwoods known for their strength and long life.

Why Homeowners Choose It

  • Exceptional hardness and durability
  • Naturally resists rot and insects
  • A rich, high-end look
  • Among the longest-lasting wood options

Good For

  • Homeowners who want a premium wood deck that lasts
  • High-traffic decks that see heavy use
  • Owners who will oil it to keep the color, or are happy with silver-gray aging

Lifespan & Upkeep

  • Lifespan: about 25 years or more
  • Upkeep: oil periodically to keep the rich color, or let it weather to silver-gray
  • Cost: the highest of the wood options

Keep in Mind

  • Very dense, so it takes longer to cut and fasten, which adds labor
  • Highest upfront cost of the woods
  • Ask where the wood comes from, since sustainable sourcing matters

Composite Decking

Made from a blend of wood fibers and plastic, composite is built to look good with very little upkeep.

Why Homeowners Choose It

  • No staining, sealing, or sanding
  • Resists rot, insects, and splinters
  • Wide range of colors and wood-grain finishes
  • Many brands come with 25-year or longer warranties

Good For

  • Homeowners who would rather enjoy the deck than maintain it
  • Busy households and families with kids and pets
  • Decks you want to look the same for years

Lifespan & Upkeep

  • Lifespan: about 20 to 30 years or more, depending on the brand
  • Upkeep: wash with soap and water a couple of times a year, and clear debris from between boards
  • Cost: higher upfront than wood, with less spent on upkeep later

Keep in Mind

  • Higher upfront cost than wood
  • Gets hotter in direct sun, especially darker colors
  • Lower-end boards can scratch, fade, or grow mold in damp shade, so brand and quality matter

PVC Decking

All-plastic decking that is lightweight and highly resistant to moisture.

Why Homeowners Choose It

  • Highly resistant to moisture, rot, and insects
  • Low maintenance, cleaned with soap and water
  • Resists scratches and fading
  • Lightweight, with a wide range of colors

Good For

  • Wet, humid, or heavily shaded spots
  • Decks near pools or water
  • Homeowners who want the lowest upkeep available

Lifespan & Upkeep

  • Lifespan: many brands carry long limited warranties, often 25 years or more
  • Upkeep: wash a couple of times a year, with no sealing or staining
  • Cost: usually the highest upfront cost of the options here

Keep in Mind

  • Highest upfront cost
  • Can't be stained or refinished, so choose a color you'll love
  • Darker colors get hot in direct sun

Deck Questions

Is composite worth the extra cost?

Composite costs more upfront, but you skip the staining and sealing that wood needs every few years. Whether it pays off depends on how long you plan to keep the home and how much you want to maintain. We'll talk through it with you.

How soon can I stain a new pressure-treated deck?

New treated lumber holds a lot of moisture, and many builders recommend letting it dry for several weeks before staining. We'll tell you the right timing for your deck.

Do composite and PVC decks get hot?

They can in direct sun, especially darker colors. Lighter colors stay cooler, and wood like cedar tends to feel cooler underfoot. If bare feet matter, we'll help you pick accordingly.

Can you build porches and covered spaces too?

Yes. Tell us what you have in mind, whether that's a covered deck, a screened porch, or a pergola, and we'll talk through the options.

Not sure which material to choose?

That's what we're here for. Call or email, tell us your budget and how much upkeep you want, and we'll recommend what fits.
