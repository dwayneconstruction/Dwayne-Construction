Decking Materials at a Glance

The material you choose changes how your deck looks, how long it lasts, and how much time you spend maintaining it. Here is how the most common options compare.

MaterialTypical lifespanUpkeepUpfront costBest for
Pressure-Treated PineAbout 10 to 20 yearsHigh: seal or stain every 2 to 3 yearsLowestTighter budgets and large decks
CedarAbout 15 to 30 yearsModerate to high: refinish periodically, or let it weatherModerateA natural wood look that stays cooler underfoot
CompositeAbout 20 to 30 years or moreLow: wash a few times a yearHigherLow upkeep with lots of color choices
PVCLong warranties, often 25 years or moreLow: wash a few times a yearHighestWet, humid, or shaded decks

Lifespans are general estimates. Actual results depend on installation, sun and moisture exposure, and how well the deck is maintained.

Deck Questions

Is composite worth the extra cost?

Composite costs more upfront, but you skip the staining and sealing that wood needs every few years. Whether it pays off depends on how long you plan to keep the home and how much you want to maintain. We'll talk through it with you.

How soon can I stain a new pressure-treated deck?

New treated lumber holds a lot of moisture, and many builders recommend letting it dry for several weeks before staining. We'll tell you the right timing for your deck.

Do composite and PVC decks get hot?

They can in direct sun, especially darker colors. Lighter colors stay cooler, and wood like cedar tends to feel cooler underfoot. If bare feet matter, we'll help you pick accordingly.

Can you build porches and covered spaces too?

Yes. Tell us what you have in mind, whether that's a covered deck, a screened porch, or a pergola, and we'll talk through the options.

Not sure which material to choose?

That's what we're here for. Call or email, tell us your budget and how much upkeep you want, and we'll recommend what fits.
